"AI agent" is one of those terms that gets used to mean everything from a simple chatbot to a fully autonomous system. For the purposes of running a real business, here is the only definition that matters:

**An AI agent is a system that receives inputs, decides what to do, and takes actions — without a human in the loop for each step.**

That last part is what separates agents from tools. A tool does something when you tell it to. An agent monitors for conditions and acts on them automatically.

## A concrete example

A missed-lead recovery agent does the following entirely on its own:

1. Watches for new form fills, voicemails, or messages in your business's communication channels
2. Detects a new inquiry and triggers a response workflow
3. Sends a personalized first message to the customer within minutes
4. Asks qualifying questions and interprets the answers
5. Summarizes the lead — what they need, their urgency, key details — and routes it to the right person
6. Follows up on a schedule if the customer goes quiet

No human presses a button. No one has to be watching a dashboard. The agent handles the top of that workflow continuously, even at 2am on a Sunday.

## What "managed" means

Running an AI agent is not a one-time setup. The prompts need tuning as you learn what customers ask. The integrations need maintenance as tools change. Edge cases come up that need decisions. New workflows need to be added as your operations grow.

Managed agent ops means someone is responsible for all of that — not just the initial build. You are buying an ongoing operations function, not a piece of software.

## Why this is not just for big companies

Three years ago, running systems like this required a dedicated AI engineering team. The infrastructure was expensive, the models were unreliable, and the integrations were brittle.

That has changed. The combination of reliable large language models, purpose-built agent runtimes, and composable integration layers means a small business can run production-quality AI agent operations for a fraction of what it would have cost before.

The businesses winning on this right now are not big enterprises. They are owner-operated service businesses that moved early — and are now recovering leads their competitors are still losing.
